20 Minutes

The 6 Major Contemporary Perspectives

Explore how today's psychologists examine human behavior and mental processes from complementary viewpoints.

Biological / Neuroscience

Focus: Brain & Body

Key Topics: Neurotransmitters, Genetics, Brain Anatomy

Examines how body chemistry, brain structures, hormonal fluctuations, and genetic inheritances create our thoughts, feelings, and behaviors.

Key Question: How do brain circuits and chemical messengers regulate emotion and memory?

Cognitive Perspective

Focus: Information Processing

Key Figures: Jean Piaget, Aaron Beck, Noam Chomsky

Investigates how we encode, process, store, and retrieve information. Views the human mind similarly to a sophisticated computer processor.

Key Question: How do thinking patterns, problem-solving, and beliefs distort our view of reality?

Humanistic Perspective

Focus: Growth & Free Will

Key Figures: Carl Rogers, Abraham Maslow

Stresses human potential, personal growth, self-actualization, and individual free choice, viewing individuals as inherently striving toward fulfillment.

Key Question: How do unconditional positive regard and self-concept drive human flourishing?

Evolutionary Perspective

Focus: Adaptation & Survival

Key Figure: David Buss (based on Charles Darwin)

Analyzes how natural selection favoured specific psychological traits and evolutionary mechanisms that aided human survival and reproduction over millennia.

Key Question: How does natural selection explain universal human behaviors like mate choice or fear of snakes?

Sociocultural Perspective

Focus: Context & Environment

Key Figure: Lev Vygotsky

Examines how behavior, cognitive processing, and attitudes vary across different cultures, social groups, norms, and environmental conditions.

Key Question: How do cultural expectations and social norms shape personality and moral reasoning?

Psychodynamic & Learning

Focus: Evolved Roots

Key Figures: Neo-Freudians & Applied Behavioral Analysts

Modern adaptations focusing on interpersonal relations, attachment theories (Psychodynamic), and observable environmental reinforcement schedules (Behavioral).

Key Question: How do early attachment styles or learned habits influence adult choices?
